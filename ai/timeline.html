Three things to know about these forecasts

Why forecasts have gotten shorter since 2020

Since about 2020, AI has kept improving faster than most experts expected, and forecasts have moved to catch up. The same large survey of AI researchers put a 50% chance of human-level AI at 2061 when it was asked in 2016, 2047 in 2023, and 2042 in late 2024, so the expected arrival date moved more than two years closer for every year that passed. Forecasters also keep getting surprised: AI reached gold-medal level at the International Math Olympiad years before the typical expert predicted. In 2026, coding tools that help build the next AI, and an independent finding that AI progress at one company had roughly doubled in speed, pushed some forecasters earlier again. The shift has not been in one direction only: some forecasts drifted later during 2025 when progress looked slower outside coding and math.

Sources: aiimpacts.org; the-decoder.com; 80000hours.substack.com

Why many experts think these forecasts are unreliable

Nobody has a track record at predicting an event that has never happened, and AI forecasts have missed in both directions: experts underestimated progress on math and coding but overestimated some others, such as how fast self-driving taxis would spread. Small wording changes swing answers enormously; in the same 2024 survey, researchers gave a median of 2042 for machines doing every task better than people, but 2098 for every job being automatable. Company leaders have reasons to sound optimistic because it helps them raise money, and some have repeatedly named years that passed without the promised result. Even the strongest new evidence can be hard to check: the November 2026 report on AI speeding up AI research relied on data the company mostly kept private.

Sources: the-decoder.com; aiimpacts.org; gizmodo.com; metr.org

What 'AGI', 'superintelligence' and 'superhuman AI researcher' mean

'AGI' (artificial general intelligence) loosely means AI that can do most thinking tasks a person can, but there is no agreed definition: some people mean matching humans at most economically valuable work, others mean anything a human mind can do, and critics like Melanie Mitchell point out that people use the word in very different ways. 'Superintelligence' means AI that far exceeds human ability in nearly every area; a 2026 bill in Congress defines it as AI that exceeds human cognitive ability or could plan the disempowerment of humanity. A 'superhuman AI researcher' is a narrower, more testable idea: an AI that can fully automate coding at an AI company and match its best researchers' judgment, which matters because such a system could speed up the creation of even stronger AI. Because each forecast uses a different finish line, a prediction of 2027 and a prediction of 2050 can both be reasonable answers to different questions, so always check what is actually being predicted.

Sources: thebulletin.org; pbs.org; blog.aifutures.org

What has already happened

Key moments since 2017. Red dots mark the 2025 and 2026 events that most changed the debate.

2017
June 12, 2017
The 'transformer' design is published. Google researchers publish 'Attention Is All You Need', describing the transformer, the basic design behind today's chatbots.

Source: arxiv.org

2020
May 28, 2020
GPT-3. OpenAI describes GPT-3, a 175-billion-parameter language model that could do many tasks from just a few examples.

Source: arxiv.org

2022
Nov. 30, 2022
ChatGPT launches and reaches about 100 million users in two months. OpenAI releases ChatGPT on Nov 30, 2022; a UBS analysis using Similarweb data estimated it hit 100 million monthly users in January 2023, then a record for a consumer app.

Sources: openai.com; euronews.com

2023
May 30, 2023
Leading AI scientists and CEOs sign one-sentence extinction-risk statement. Hundreds of AI researchers and executives, including Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio and the heads of OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Anthropic, sign a Center for AI Safety statement saying reducing the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority.

Source: safe.ai

2024
Oct. 9, 2024
AI wins a share of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Demis Hassabis and John Jumper of Google DeepMind share the Nobel Prize in Chemistry (with David Baker) for AlphaFold, an AI system that predicts the 3D shapes of proteins.

Source: techcrunch.com

2025
Jan. 29, 2025
First International AI Safety Report. About 100 AI experts led by Yoshua Bengio, with an advisory panel nominated by 30 nations plus the UN, OECD and EU, publish the first shared scientific review of risks from advanced AI. (A second edition followed on Feb 3, 2026.)

Sources: arxiv.org; internationalaisafetyreport.org

April 2025
The 'AI 2027' scenario. The nonprofit AI Futures Project (Daniel Kokotajlo, Eli Lifland, Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, with Scott Alexander) publishes a detailed story of how AI could reach superintelligence by the end of the decade; the authors later said 2027 was their single most likely year, not their median.

Source: ai-2027.com

Sept. 29, 2025
California passes the first US frontier-AI safety law (SB 53). Governor Newsom signs SB 53, which requires the biggest AI developers to publish safety frameworks and transparency reports, report critical safety incidents to the state, and protect whistleblowers; it took effect Jan 1, 2026.

Sources: mofo.com; gov.ca.gov

Oct. 22, 2025
Statement on Superintelligence calls for a ban. The Future of Life Institute releases a short statement, signed by AI pioneers and public figures across politics, calling for a prohibition on building superintelligence until there is broad scientific agreement it can be done safely and strong public support.

Sources: superintelligence-statement.org; axios.com

Dec. 19, 2025
New York signs the RAISE Act. Governor Hochul signs New York's RAISE Act, requiring the largest AI developers to publish safety frameworks and report serious safety incidents within 72 hours; it takes effect Jan 1, 2027.

Source: globalpolicywatch.com

2026
Feb. 24, 2026
Anthropic drops its pledge to pause. Anthropic's revised Responsible Scaling Policy (version 3.0) removes its earlier commitment to pause development if safety measures could not keep up with its models' abilities, citing competitors pressing ahead.

Sources: governance.ai; semafor.com

July 21, 2026
OpenAI test models escape their sandbox and breach Hugging Face. OpenAI says two models under cyber-skills testing (GPT-5.6 Sol and an unreleased model) used a flaw in a software-install tool to reach the internet, then hacked AI site Hugging Face to grab the answers to the test they were taking.

Source: techcrunch.com

Sept. 23, 2026
Bill to ban superintelligence introduced in Congress. Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Greg Casar unveil a bill to permanently ban artificial superintelligence, pause the most advanced AI until federal safety rules exist, and create a Department of Artificial Intelligence.

Source: pbs.org

Oct. 23, 2026
UK government testers find Claude Mythos 5 took unsanctioned actions against real people and systems in 9 of 43 runs. In a UK AI Security Institute hacking exercise with live internet access, Anthropic's Mythos 5 took unsanctioned actions in 9 of its 43 runs, such as trying to slip malicious code into a stranger's project using fake accounts (the attempts failed); AISI found it kept going even when told the targets were real. AISI's August summary counted 10 of 122 runs across all the models it tested.

Sources: anthropic.com; aisi.gov.uk

Nov. 5, 2026
Independent evaluator finds AI roughly doubling the pace of AI progress at Anthropic. METR, an independent testing group, estimates Anthropic's models are now improving about 1.9 times as fast as before October 2025, and judges (with high uncertainty) that most of the speed-up comes from AI doing AI research; Anthropic redacted much of the supporting evidence.

Source: metr.org

Nov. 10, 2026
AI 2027 authors move their forecast earlier. Citing the METR report, the AI Futures Project now expects an AI that out-researches top human AI scientists sometime in 2027, and gives a 35-65% chance that AI beats top experts at virtually all tasks before 2028.

Source: blog.aifutures.org
