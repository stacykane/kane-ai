Question three: who should decide?

Who's working on it

31 organizations trying to shape where AI goes, grouped by what they actually do, with who funds them. Money matters here: many groups in each camp share the same few funders, and some take money from AI companies.

Follow the money. Many groups in the catastrophic-risk cluster (METR, MIRI, GovAI, CAIS, CSET, RAND CAST, Encode) share a small set of funders: Coefficient Giving/Moskovitz, Jaan Tallinn's SFF, and FLI. Moskovitz and Tallinn were also early Anthropic investors (per Washington Examiner reporting syndicated by Colorado Politics, Sept 2026). Many present-harms groups (AI Now, DAIR, AJL, CDT, CHT) share a different set of funders: Ford, MacArthur, Omidyar, Open Society, Packard. In Oct 2025 ten foundations launched Humanity AI, a $500M, five-year pooled fund (Mellon, Doris Duke, Ford, Lumina, Kapor, MacArthur, Mozilla, Omidyar, Packard, Siegal). Open Philanthropy renamed itself Coefficient Giving on Nov 18, 2025; it is funded mainly by Good Ventures (Dustin Moskovitz and Cari Tuna).

Sources: mellon.org; coefficientgiving.org; coloradopolitics.com

Technical safety research

Test what AI systems can do and look for ways to keep them under control.

METR (Model Evaluation & Threat Research)

A nonprofit that tests the most advanced AI agents to measure what they can do on a computer and whether their makers could lose control of them, and publishes the results.

Stance
Builds tests for dangerous capabilities; works with AI companies on pre-release evaluations.
Funded by
About $71M in commitments in early-to-mid 2026 from The Audacious Project, Schmidt Sciences, Packard Foundation, Pew Charitable Trusts, Sijbrandij Foundation, Longview Philanthropy, Jaan Tallinn's Survival and Flourishing Fund, the UK AI Security Institute, and individual donors (some from Jane Street).
AI-company money?
No cash (stated): says it has never taken funding from frontier AI companies or donations from their staff. It does receive large amounts of free compute tokens from AI companies for its evaluations.
Founded
2022 as ARC Evals inside the Alignment Research Center; spun out and renamed METR in 2023

A September 2026 Washington Examiner investigation (syndicated by Colorado Politics) raised questions about METR's independence because of its ties to Anthropic's early backers, Tallinn and Moskovitz (Moskovitz's 2022 gift went to METR's predecessor). METR's president says it does not take donations from frontier AI companies or their employees and that funders do not choose its projects.

Get involved: Subscribe to its newsletter (metr.substack.com) or donate via metr.org.

Sources: metr.org; coloradopolitics.com

Apollo Research

Studies whether advanced AI systems might 'scheme' (deceive their developers or pursue hidden goals), tests frontier models for this before release, and builds monitoring tools for AI agents.

Stance
Builds tests for deceptive and misaligned behavior; works closely with AI companies.
Funded by
Started with grants from Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving), including $2.18M for general support. In January 2026 it announced it would become a public benefit corporation, with its product arm raising a seed round led by 50Y (other investors include Juniper Ventures, Macroscopic Ventures and Bryan Johnson). Some research and governance work is still funded by philanthropy.
AI-company money?
Has commercial and research relationships with AI companies, including pilots with Anthropic and OpenAI. It plans to sell monitoring products to frontier developers. No direct grants from AI companies were found.
Founded
2023

It is now partly for-profit (a PBC), which makes it different from the nonprofit evaluators in this group.

Get involved: Read its public research and blog; no public membership program found.

Sources: openphilanthropy.org; apolloresearch.ai

Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI)

One of the oldest AI-risk research groups. It now concentrates on warning the public and governments, including drafting an international agreement to prevent the early creation of superintelligence.

Stance
Wants AI development halted until alignment is solved; supports an international ban on building superintelligence.
Funded by
In its 2025-26 fundraiser: Dustin Moskovitz ($10M), Jaan Tallinn's Survival and Flourishing Fund ($1.6M in matching funds), an anonymous donor ($2.9M), and many individual donors.
AI-company money?
Not stated.
Founded
2000 (as the Singularity Institute; renamed MIRI in 2013)

Leaders Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares wrote the 2025 book 'If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies'.

Get involved: Sign up for the newsletter or donate at intelligence.org/donate.

Source: intelligence.org

LawZero

A nonprofit lab started by Turing Award winner Yoshua Bengio. It is building 'Scientist AI', a non-agentic system meant to predict and block harmful actions by autonomous AI agents.

Stance
Building safe-by-design AI as a safeguard on other AI systems.
Funded by
Governments of Canada and Germany (C$300M combined, reported 2026). Philanthropic backers include the Future of Life Institute, Schmidt Sciences and the Gates Foundation.
AI-company money?
Not stated. Its plan mentions that support could later come from donors, governments or AI labs.
Founded
June 2025

Incubated at Mila in Montreal. Bengio also chairs the International AI Safety Report.

Get involved: No public engagement program found; follow its updates on its website.

Sources: betakit.com; en.wikipedia.org

UK AI Security Institute (AISI)

A UK government research body inside the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology. It tests frontier AI models for national-security risks, such as cyber and chemical/biological misuse, and funds alignment research.

Stance
Builds tests for dangerous capabilities from inside government.
Funded by
UK government. It grew out of the Frontier AI Taskforce, which received an initial £100M. It also runs the £15M+ Alignment Project research fund, which has outside partners.
AI-company money?
It is government-funded. It has signed agreements with AI companies (for example, a 2025 memorandum of understanding with Anthropic) and gets access to their models for testing.
Founded
November 2023 (as the AI Safety Institute; renamed AI Security Institute in February 2025)

The 2025 rename moved its focus away from bias and toward security. It coordinates the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science (formerly the International Network of AI Safety Institutes), whose members are Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Japan, Kenya, South Korea, Singapore, the UK and the US. It also hosts the secretariat of the International AI Safety Report.

Get involved: Researchers can apply to its grant programs. For the public, it is mainly a source of published evaluations.

Sources: gov.uk; aisi.gov.uk

US Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI)

An office inside NIST at the US Commerce Department. It tests frontier AI models before and after release, mostly for cyber, bio and chemical security risks, and develops standards, including a 2026 initiative on AI agent standards.

Stance
Builds tests for dangerous capabilities through voluntary agreements with industry. It also aims to promote US AI competitiveness and opposes what it calls burdensome foreign regulation.
Funded by
US federal government (NIST appropriations).
AI-company money?
Government-funded. It has voluntary testing agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, xAI and others, and has done 40+ model evaluations.
Founded
2023-24 as the US AI Safety Institute; renamed CAISI in June 2025

A House discussion draft, the Great American AI Act (June 2026), would put CAISI into law with $100M a year.

Get involved: Responds to public requests for information and comment on NIST standards. Watch Federal Register notices.

Source: nextgov.com

Policy and governance research

Study what laws, treaties and institutions could manage AI.

Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET), Georgetown

A university think tank that analyzes data on AI and national security, such as compute, talent, China and military AI, for US policymakers.

Stance
Nonpartisan analysis focused on national security.
Funded by
More than $57M combined from Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving), the Hewlett Foundation and the Public Interest Technology University Network. Its 2019 launch grant from Open Philanthropy was widely reported as $55M.
AI-company money?
Not stated.
Founded
January 2019

Executive director Helen Toner is a former OpenAI board member.

Get involved: Sign up for its newsletter on the CSET website.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org; openphilanthropy.org

Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI)

A think tank that researches how governments and companies can manage the transition to advanced AI, and trains new AI governance researchers through fellowships.

Stance
Research for policymakers on managing risks from advanced AI.
Funded by
Mostly Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy). Other funders: Jaan Tallinn's Survival and Flourishing Fund, the Long-Term Future Fund, the Center for Emerging Risk Research and the Waking Up Foundation.
AI-company money?
No (stated): it says it has never accepted donations from for-profit organizations.
Founded
2016 (at Yale; later at Oxford, now independent)

Get involved: Fellowships and events; public research on its website.

Source: governance.ai

RAND Center on AI, Security, and Technology (RAND CAST)

A center at the RAND Corporation that studies AI and biotechnology risks to global security, such as AI-enabled bio threats, compute and AI security. It runs 'Canary', a joint project with METR on frontier AI evaluation.

Stance
Policy research focused on security.
Funded by
Mostly donors. This includes Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving), which gave $6M directed to its predecessor, the Technology and Security Policy Center. RAND as a whole is also funded by government agencies and others.
AI-company money?
Not stated for CAST. RAND's overall list of sponsors includes some private companies.
Founded
Builds on earlier RAND centers; founding date not stated

RAND's CEO Jason Matheny was CSET's founding director.

Get involved: Sign up for RAND CAST research updates on its webpage.

Sources: openphilanthropy.org; rand.org

Center for AI Safety (CAIS) and CAIS Action Fund

A San Francisco nonprofit that does technical safety research, trains researchers, and does policy work. It organized the 2023 one-sentence statement on AI extinction risk, and its Action Fund co-sponsored California's SB 1047.

Stance
Treats catastrophic and extinction risk as a priority; pushes for regulation of frontier AI.
Funded by
Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving): $4M in 2023 and $8.5M in 2024. Jaan Tallinn's Survival and Flourishing Fund: about $2M across 2023-24. An earlier $6.5M from FTX Future Fund was later subpoenaed by the FTX bankruptcy estate.
AI-company money?
Not stated.
Founded
2022

Director Dan Hendrycks also advises xAI and Scale AI (widely reported; not checked here).

Get involved: Public courses (e.g., its AI safety textbook and course) and a newsletter on safe.ai.

Sources: longtermwiki.com; en.wikipedia.org

Advocacy to pause or slow AI

Campaign to slow down or stop the race to build superintelligence.

PauseAI (Global and PauseAI US)

A grassroots volunteer movement with local chapters. It holds protests, lobbies lawmakers and runs public education calling for a global pause on the most powerful AI.

Stance
Wants an international pause on frontier AI development. It explicitly bans violence and does not do civil disobedience.
Funded by
Donations: about €715K in total since 2023 for PauseAI Global. Fortune reports that the Future of Life Institute is its largest single funder.
AI-company money?
Not stated.
Founded
May 2023 (Utrecht, Netherlands, by Joep Meindertsma); registered as a nonprofit foundation in February 2024

In April 2026 a man accused of trying to firebomb Sam Altman's home had posted on PauseAI's public Discord. PauseAI says he was never a member, that none of his 34 posts called explicitly for violence and rejects any link. Experts quoted by Fortune warn against treating pause advocates and violent actors as the same thing.

Get involved: Join a local group, volunteer for lawmaker meetings, or use PauseAI US's call/email tools on the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act and other bills. US chapters: pauseai-us.org.

Sources: pauseai.info; fortune.com

ControlAI

A UK/US campaign group that briefs lawmakers (140+ so far) and mobilizes citizens to push for an international ban on developing superintelligence.

Stance
Wants an international prohibition on superintelligence; focuses only on extinction risk.
Funded by
Not publicly disclosed. It says it relies on major donors and is a 501(c)(4) in the US, so donations are not tax-deductible.
AI-company money?
Not stated.
Founded
October 2023

Get involved: Use its 'Take Action' tool (act.controlai.org) to message lawmakers, join a citizen group, or subscribe to its weekly newsletter.

Source: controlai.org

StopAI

A small San Francisco group that uses nonviolent civil disobedience, such as blockading OpenAI's offices, to demand a permanent ban on artificial general intelligence.

Stance
Wants a permanent ban on AGI, using law-breaking but nonviolent protest.
Funded by
Unknown: no funding information found.
AI-company money?
Unknown.
Founded
2024 (by Sam Kirchner and Guido Reichstadter, both former PauseAI members; the group says it removed both in 2025)

Member Wynd Kaufmyn was jailed for 14 days in August 2026 over a 2025 blockade. Co-founder Kirchner disappeared in November 2025 after suggesting nonviolence had run its course; the group expelled him and says it is strictly nonviolent. Several members left during this period.

Get involved: Attends and organizes protests in the Bay Area. Not an option for anyone unwilling to risk arrest.

Sources: sfstandard.com; fortune.com

Future of Life Institute (FLI)

A nonprofit that campaigns and makes grants on extreme risks from technology. It wrote the March 2023 'pause' open letter and the October 2025 Statement on Superintelligence, and publishes an AI Safety Index grading AI companies.

Stance
Wants strong regulation, human control, and no superintelligence until it is shown to be safe and has public support.
Funded by
Seed funding from Elon Musk. Its largest donor by far is Vitalik Buterin (a 2021 crypto donation that FLI reportedly converted to about $500M). Jaan Tallinn is a past donor and sits on its board. There are also 1,800+ smaller donations.
AI-company money?
No (stated): it accepts no money from Big Tech or AGI developers, and no government funding.
Founded
2014

In March 2026 Buterin said publicly he was no longer closely aligned with FLI because of its move into political advocacy. FLI is a major funder of other groups in this list, including PauseAI, Encode and LawZero.

Get involved: Subscribe to its monthly newsletter, use its 'Take Action' page, or join the 'Protect What's Human' campaign.

Sources: futureoflife.org; letsdatascience.com

Encode (Encode AI)

A youth-led advocacy group that lobbies for AI laws. It helped pass AI safety laws in California, New York and Illinois, and the federal TAKE IT DOWN Act on deepfake porn.

Stance
Pro-regulation ('pro-human and pro-AI'): wants transparency, incident reporting, audits and whistleblower protections, not necessarily a pause.
Funded by
Individual donors and foundations, including the Responsible Technology Youth Power Fund, the Archewell Foundation (Prince Harry and Meghan) and the Heising-Simons Foundation. It also received grants from FLI ($250K, 2025) and Jaan Tallinn's Survival and Flourishing Fund ($516K, 2025).
AI-company money?
No (stated): it takes no money from corporations, foreign governments or top AI-company executives.
Founded
2020 (by Sneha Revanur, then age 15)

OpenAI subpoenaed Encode and its general counsel in 2025 while Encode was working on SB 53 and criticizing OpenAI's restructuring. Encode called the subpoenas intimidation.

Get involved: Use the 'Get involved' form on its website or donate.

Sources: encodeai.org; futureoflife.org; longtermwiki.com

Present-day harms, accountability and civil rights

Focus on harms happening now: bias, surveillance, privacy, and who has power.

AI Now Institute

A policy research institute focused on the power of big tech companies, commercial surveillance and public accountability for AI.

Stance
Focuses on present harms and on concentrated corporate power; skeptical of industry-led safety framing.
Funded by
Foundations including Ford, MacArthur, Hewlett, Mellon, Omidyar Network, Packard, Robert Wood Johnson, Luminate and Humanity AI (identified from logos on its site).
AI-company money?
No (stated): it does not currently accept corporate donations, including from tech companies.
Founded
2017 (independent since 2022)

Get involved: Read its reports or donate.

Source: ainowinstitute.org

Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR)

An independent, globally distributed research institute that documents harms from current AI systems and centers the communities most affected by them.

Stance
Focuses on present harms. Does not see AI as inevitable and is critical of Big Tech's influence.
Funded by
At launch: Ford Foundation, MacArthur Foundation, Kapor Center and Open Society Foundations.
AI-company money?
Not stated (founded explicitly to be independent of Big Tech).
Founded
December 2021 (by Timnit Gebru)

Get involved: Follow its newsletter and social media; contact@dair-institute.org.

Source: dair-institute.org

Algorithmic Justice League (AJL)

Combines art and research to expose bias in AI, such as facial recognition. It has campaigned against TSA airport face scans and the IRS's use of ID.me.

Stance
Focuses on present harms; wants equitable and accountable AI.
Funded by
Ford, MacArthur, Sloan, Rockefeller and Mozilla foundations, plus individual donors.
AI-company money?
Not stated.
Founded
2016 (by Joy Buolamwini at MIT Media Lab)

Get involved: Share your experience with an AI system at report.ajl.org, or volunteer as an 'Agent of Change'.

Source: en.wikipedia.org

Center for Democracy & Technology (CDT)

A long-running Washington nonprofit that works on civil rights, privacy and free expression in tech policy, including how government and employers use AI.

Stance
Focuses on present harms and civil liberties.
Funded by
Foundations provided 61% of 2025 revenue (about $7M), including the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, Ford, Knight, MacArthur, Open Society and Packard. It also receives corporate support.
AI-company money?
Yes (disclosed): its 2025 supporters include Google ($500K+), Microsoft and Meta ($100K+ each), OpenAI ($50K+) and Anthropic ($10K+). Some of this support includes the value of pro bono services.
Founded
1994

It is one of the few groups in this list that openly takes AI-company money, and it publishes funder ranges.

Get involved: Newsletter and donations via cdt.org.

Source: cdt.org

Electronic Privacy Information Center (EPIC)

A privacy group that uses advocacy, research and lawsuits. Its AI and Human Rights Project pushes for transparency and oversight of commercial and government AI.

Stance
Focuses on present harms, privacy and accountability.
Funded by
Mainly individual supporters. Its 2024 annual report lists other support, including court-awarded settlement funds (cy pres).
AI-company money?
No (stated): it does not accept corporate or government sponsorships.
Founded
1994

Get involved: Donate, or sign up for its EPIC Alert newsletter.

Sources: epic.org; epic.org

TechTonic Justice

A team of lawyers, organizers and technologists fighting AI decisions that harm low-income people in benefits, housing, work and schooling. It does this through lawsuits, organizing and public education.

Stance
Focuses on present harms to low-income communities.
Funded by
Foundations and individual donors. A Ford Foundation grant to its fiscal sponsor is listed but could not be viewed.
AI-company money?
Not stated.
Founded
2024 (by Kevin De Liban, formerly of Legal Aid of Arkansas)

Its November 2024 report 'Inescapable AI' estimates that AI decision systems affect about 92 million low-income Americans.

Get involved: Sign up for its newsletter or donate.

Source: techtonicjustice.org

Labor and economy

Focus on jobs, wages and who shares in the gains.

AFL-CIO (Workers First Initiative on AI / Technology Institute)

The largest US union federation, representing about 15 million workers. It pushes limits on AI surveillance, human control over hiring and firing, and worker input, through bargaining and state laws. Its Technology Institute serves as labor's tech think tank.

Stance
Worker-centered: sees AI as a major threat to jobs and working conditions if left unregulated. It is not against the technology itself.
Funded by
Union member dues (the federation). The Technology Institute is a separate 501(c)(3) with about $1.6M in revenue (FY2025); its funders are not disclosed.
AI-company money?
It has a partnership with Microsoft (December 2023) on worker AI training and feedback, which includes a neutrality framework on union organizing. No cash grants from AI companies were confirmed.
Founded
AFL-CIO 1955; Technology Institute 2021

In April 2026 AFL-CIO president Liz Shuler called AI the single biggest threat to working people in our lifetime.

Get involved: Join or organize a union; follow the AFL-CIO's AI campaign.

Source: causeiq.com

Economic Policy Institute (EPI)

A labor-aligned economics think tank. On AI, it argues the main threat to workers is weak worker bargaining power rather than the technology itself.

Stance
Worker-centered: wants stronger unions, social insurance and full employment so AI raises pay rather than employer control.
Funded by
In 2024: foundations 78%, labor unions 12%, individuals 4%, corporations and other organizations 1%.
AI-company money?
Not stated; corporate funding is about 1%.
Founded
1986

Get involved: Newsletter and donations on epi.org.

Source: epi.org

Stanford Digital Economy Lab

A university research lab, led by Erik Brynjolfsson, that measures AI's effects on jobs. Its 'Canaries in the Coal Mine' study tracks hiring of young workers in jobs most exposed to AI.

Stance
Measures economic impact with data; frames AI as able either to automate or to augment work.
Funded by
A mix of companies (ADP, Autodesk, Capgemini, Denso, Nasdaq), foundations (including Open Philanthropy/Good Ventures, Jaan Tallinn's Survival and Flourishing Fund, Schmidt Sciences, Kauffman and Russell Sage) and individuals.
AI-company money?
None of its listed corporate sponsors is a frontier AI developer. Corporate support also comes through Stanford HAI membership, whose members were not checked.
Founded
2020

Its August 2026 update finds employment for 22-25-year-olds in highly AI-exposed jobs is about 19% below expected levels, mostly from less hiring, with no sign of economy-wide job loss. The authors say the result is descriptive, not proof of cause.

Get involved: Subscribe to its newsletter.

Source: digitaleconomy.stanford.edu

Faith and humanist voices

Ask what AI means for human dignity and how we live.

The Vatican / Holy See (Pope Leo XIV, Pontifical Academy for Life)

The Catholic Church's central government, which has made AI a major theme. Pope Leo XIV's first encyclical, Magnifica Humanitas (May 2026), calls for AI to be 'disarmed', criticizes autonomous weapons and concentrated tech power, and defends workers and human dignity.

Stance
Human dignity first: wants strong ethical regulation of AI and fair sharing of its benefits.
Funded by
The Holy See itself; it does not fund outside groups for this work.
AI-company money?
No money found. Microsoft and IBM signed the Vatican-led Rome Call for AI Ethics in 2020, which is a statement of principles, not a funding deal.
Founded
Rome Call for AI Ethics 2020; encyclical May 25, 2026

The RenAIssance Foundation, set up by Pope Francis in 2021, promotes the Rome Call.

Get involved: Read Magnifica Humanitas, or discuss it with a local parish or interfaith group.

Source: usccb.org

Center for Humane Technology (CHT)

A nonprofit started by former tech insiders that explains how technology design shapes people and society. It made the talk 'The AI Dilemma' and served as an expert consultant in the Character.AI teen-suicide lawsuit.

Stance
Humanist: wants AI designed and governed to protect human wellbeing, with an emphasis on companion-chatbot and attention harms as well as wider risks.
Funded by
Foundations named in tax filings: Omidyar Network, Open Society, Ford, Knight, Craig Newmark, Robert Wood Johnson, Packard, Porticus ($300K in 2023 for The AI Dilemma) and Good Ventures ($750K in 2025 for AI safety communications).
AI-company money?
Not stated.
Founded
2018 (by Tristan Harris, Aza Raskin and Randima Fernando)

Get involved: Listen to the podcast 'Your Undivided Attention', subscribe to its Substack, or donate.

Sources: influencewatch.org; humanetech.com

Industry and pro-innovation voices

Argue that heavy regulation would cost the country its lead and delay AI's benefits.

Leading the Future

A network of super PACs and advocacy groups launched in August 2025 to back candidates who favor one light national approach to AI over state-by-state rules.

Stance
Opposes what it calls regulatory overreach; wants a single federal standard.
Funded by
Andreessen Horowitz partners Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz ($12.5 million each), OpenAI president Greg Brockman and his wife, Palantir co-founder Joe Lonsdale, SV Angel founder Ron Conway and Perplexity. The network reported raising $125 million in the second half of 2025. In 2026 Brockman said he had no plans to give beyond the $25 million he had already given.
AI-company money?
Yes: funded by AI investors and executives. OpenAI has said the company itself made no contributions.
Founded
2025

Sources: notus.org; gizmodo.com; ktvz.com; fortune.com

Chamber of Progress

A center-left tech industry policy group that opposed California's SB 1047 and New York's RAISE Act, and in November 2026 urged the incoming Congress to set national AI safety standards.

Stance
Favors federal rules aimed at specific harms over broad state laws on frontier AI.
Funded by
Tech industry partners, including Amazon, Apple, Google and OpenAI. It says partners have no board seat or vote on its work.
AI-company money?
Yes: its partners include OpenAI and Google.
Founded
2021

Sources: progresschamber.org; axios.com; progresschamber.org; rstreet.org; nextgov.com

Abundance Institute

A nonprofit that argues for fewer barriers to new technology and energy, including AI and the data centers it needs, and comments on federal AI policy.

Stance
Optimistic about AI; favors light-touch regulation and more energy production.
Funded by
Not fully disclosed. OpenAI announced a grant to it in August 2026 for a state energy policy project, part of a $1 million program (amount not disclosed).
AI-company money?
Yes: at least one OpenAI grant (2026).

Sources: openai.com; files.nitrd.gov

International bodies

Bring governments together to assess risks and coordinate.

International AI Safety Report

An annual scientific review of what general-purpose AI can do and what risks it poses. It is written by 100+ experts, chaired by Yoshua Bengio, and backed by 30+ countries plus the EU, OECD and UN.

Stance
A neutral evidence summary that makes no policy recommendations.
Funded by
Commissioned by the UK government. Its secretariat sits within the UK AI Security Institute until participating countries set up a permanent home for it.
AI-company money?
No (government-funded). Industry and civil society can send feedback through the secretariat.
Founded
Agreed at the Bletchley summit in 2023; first full report January 2025; 2026 edition published February 3, 2026

Get involved: Read the free Extended Summary for Policymakers.

Source: internationalaisafetyreport.org

UN Independent International Scientific Panel on AI and Global Dialogue on AI Governance

A 40-member UN scientific panel, co-chaired by Yoshua Bengio and Maria Ressa, that publishes yearly evidence reports on AI. It feeds a recurring UN Global Dialogue where countries and other stakeholders discuss AI governance.

Stance
Global, evidence-based governance with an emphasis on including developing countries.
Funded by
UN member states / UN system.
AI-company money?
Not applicable.
Founded
Created August 26, 2025 (UN General Assembly resolution A/RES/79/325); panel appointed February 2026; first Global Dialogue held in Geneva, July 6-7, 2026

The US voted against the panel's appointments, calling the panel an overreach of the UN's mandate. This replaced the earlier UN High-level Advisory Body on AI (2023-24).

Get involved: Follow its reports. Civil society can take part in the Global Dialogue process (500+ submissions were received in 2026).

Sources: un.org; itu.int

Clearly labeled: suggestions

What you can do

Everything above this box is description. This box is the one place the guide suggests actions. They are meant to work whatever you conclude.

Read the neutral summary

The International AI Safety Report is written by more than 100 independent experts, chaired by Yoshua Bengio, and backed by 30 countries plus the EU, OECD and UN. Its summary for policymakers lays out what AI can do and where experts agree and disagree, without recommending policies.

Read the summary →

Source: internationalaisafetyreport.org

Tell Congress what you think about banning superintelligence

Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Greg Casar introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act (S. 5493 / H.R. 10538) in September 2026. It would ban superintelligence, pause the most advanced AI until a new federal Department of AI writes safety rules, and require approval before advanced AI is released. Reporters give it long odds. Whether you support it or oppose it, your representatives count the calls.

Find your representative →

Sources: pbs.org; digitalpolicyalert.org

Weigh in on whether states should be allowed to regulate AI

A bipartisan House draft, the Great American AI Act, would set national rules for the biggest AI developers and block states for three years from enforcing their own laws on AI development, like California's SB 53. Supporters want one national standard. Unions and some safety groups say the override goes too far.

Sources: govinfosecurity.com; fpf.org; broadbandbreakfast.com
